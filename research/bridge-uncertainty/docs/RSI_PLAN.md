# RSI plan

Facts below are from RSI's own pages (cee.org/programs/apply-rsi and cee.org/programs/research-science-institute), read on 10 November 2026. Re-check them when you apply; they can change.

## What RSI actually asks for
- **Who can apply:** students about to enter their last year of high school, so you apply in the middle of 11th grade. Seniors cannot apply.
- **This cycle's deadline:** applications for RSI 2027 close at 11:59 pm on 11 December 2026. If you are in 10th grade now, your cycle is the next one, a year later.
- **Essays:** your answers to the application's questions, detailing your goals in science, technology, engineering or mathematics.
- **Recommendations:** two teachers (math or science, or a research supervisor) who know you and your academic record. If you did a research project of 4 weeks or longer *at a university or a laboratory*, RSI says to request a letter from that supervisor. Three letters at most.
- **Transcript:** official high school transcript.
- **Test scores:** all PSAT, SAT, ACT and AP scores. RSI recommends PSAT Math 740 or higher and Reading and Writing 700 or higher, or ACT math 33 and verbal 34. Lower scores "must be offset by strong indicators" elsewhere. RSI strongly encourages taking the PSAT.
- **Research is not required.** RSI says some admitted students have done original research and others have completed hands-on projects or competitions.

## What that means for this project
The project feeds two parts of the application: the essays, and possibly a recommendation. It does nothing for scores, grades or the two teacher letters. Polishing the analysis further does not move any of the five items above. What moves them:

1. **PSAT and grades.** These are the stated thresholds. If your PSAT math is not yet at 740, that is worth more of your time than this project.
2. **Two teachers who know you well.** Pick your math and science teachers now. Show them this project, ask them questions about it, and let them see you work on it over the year. A teacher who watched you find and fix your own mistake can write a far stronger letter than one who only saw a finished paper.
3. **Being able to write and talk about the work yourself.** The essays ask about your goals and thinking. You can only write them well about work you understand and did.
4. **Your own experiments on top of this base.** The assistant built the base. What you add is what you can honestly call yours.

## On the AI help
Be straightforward about it wherever you describe the project. Describe what the assistant did and what you did. Selection committees, teachers and mentors respond far better to an honest account of real work than to a polished project the applicant cannot explain.
